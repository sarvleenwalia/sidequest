train the ants (evolutionary settings search)

A demo of the training loop: each generation mutates the ants' settings and plays a baseline player, and the best survive. It takes a few minutes. Four short games per candidate are a noisy training signal. Read the final report for a separate held-out evaluation.

Not trained yet.
Scores are points per game against a greedy one-move player. Checkmate/draws use chess rules; unfinished games are adjudicated after 30 half-moves using a ±200 evaluation threshold. A handful of games is noisy, so treat small differences as luck, not proof.
QuestionWhen do simple pheromone-guided agents find near-optimal routes, and can the same idea choose chess moves?
MazesPerfect mazes get extra loops. Ants walk with trail strength plus a distance-to-goal hint. Good routes deposit more. Trails fade each round. The baseline is a breadth-first search.
ChessEach ant picks a first move by trail strength and a one-move evaluation, plays a short sampled line, and scores the end position. Moves with better lines gain trail. Before the ants run, every candidate is checked against immediate captures and mate-in-one replies. This does not prove that deeper threats or hung pieces are always avoided.
TrainingA simple evolutionary loop mutates five settings and keeps the best by games won against a greedy baseline. Validation then plays trained ants against untrained ants. These are search agents, not language models or neural networks.
LimitsGames are short and few. Nonterminal positions are scored by material and centre control after 30 half-moves; these are adjudicated points, not actual game wins. The linked final report records training and held-out tests from this release; improvement is not assumed. The evaluation is material plus centre control only. This is a search experiment, not a strong engine. Mazes are simulated; no robot or real ants are measured here.
ReproduceEverything runs client-side. The shared engine, experiment seeds, raw results and protocol are in the final report. Chess rules come from the open-source chess.js. Export the comparison table as CSV and rerun with more mazes to tighten the numbers.
